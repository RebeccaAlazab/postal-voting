Voting by post

What is a postal vote?

This video explains what postal voting is, who can apply for a postal vote and what happens to your vote on polling day.

GOV.UK • 1 minutes 48 seconds

{% from "govuk/components/details/macro.njk" import govukDetails %} {% call govukDetails({ summaryText: "Transcript" }) %}

A postal vote allows you to vote without having to visit a polling station. Instead of voting in person, your ballot paper will be sent to you by post a few weeks before an election. You simply mark your choice on the ballot paper, complete and sign the postal voting statement, seal everything in the pre-paid envelope, and return it by post.

Anyone can apply, and if you’re voting in England, Scotland, or Wales, you don’t need to give a reason why you want to vote by post.

To apply for a postal vote, you must be registered to vote first. If you’re not already registered, you can do this on the GOV.UK website.

If you need a postal vote for an upcoming election, you’ll need to apply by 5pm, 11 days before polling day. Applying on GOV.UK takes a few minutes and you’ll need to know your National Insurance number, which you can find on a payslip, on letters from HMRC, or on GOV.UK by searching for ‘find my national insurance number.’ As a part of the application, you’ll also need to upload an image of your signature which will be used to verify your identity each time you vote - so make sure you have a pen and paper to hand.

Once you’ve applied, your ballot paper will arrive by post before polling day. You should complete it and post it back as quickly as possible, so that your vote gets counted. If you miss the deadline to return it, you can find out what to do on GOV.UK.

Once you post it back, your ballot paper will be securely stored and then counted alongside all the other votes, once polls close on election day.

To apply, search ‘apply postal vote’ on GOV.UK.

You must apply for a postal vote if you want to vote by post, for example if:

  • you're away from home
  • you're abroad and want to vote in England, Scotland or Wales

Apply for a postal vote

You can apply for a postal vote for one of the following:

  • a single election on a specific date
  • a specific period if you want to vote in England, Scotland or Wales
  • up to 3 years

You can:

  • apply for an online postal vote
  • apply by post for a postal vote
{% from "govuk/components/inset-text/macro.njk" import govukInsetText %} {{ govukInsetText({ text: "There’s a different form if you’re applying for a postal vote in Northern Ireland." }) }}

After you apply, you’ll be sent a postal vote ballot pack containing a ballot paper and a postal voting statement.

Change where your postal vote ballot pack is sent

Make a new application for a postal vote if you move house or you’ll be away from home when the postal vote ballot pack is sent out.

{{ govukInsetText({ text: "There’s a different form if you’re applying for a postal vote in Northern Ireland." }) }}

Completing and returning your postal vote ballot pack

When voting by post, you should:

  • mark your vote on your ballot paper in secret
  • fill in the postal voting statement
  • put the ballot paper and postal voting statement in the envelope provided
  • seal the envelope yourself
{{ govukInsetText({ text: "Return your completed postal vote ballot pack as quickly as possible to make sure your postal vote is counted." }) }}

If you’re too late to return your postal vote ballot pack by post


In England and Wales

Take your ballot paper and postal voting statement to your polling station by 10pm on election day or to your electoral registration office before it closes.

Your vote will be rejected if you do not give your ballot paper to a member of staff at the polling station or electoral registration office and complete a form.

{% from "govuk/components/warning-text/macro.njk" import govukWarningText %} {{ govukWarningText({ text: "Do not post your ballot paper through the letterbox of the electoral registration office.", iconFallbackText: "Warning" }) }}

You can hand in:

  • your own completed postal vote ballot pack
  • completed postal vote ballot packs for up to 5 other voters

If you’re a political campaigner, the 5 other voters must be family members or people you provide regular care for.

In Scotland

Return your completed postal vote ballot pack to the returning officer at your council before it closes. Find your council.

In Northern Ireland

You can only hand in your completed postal vote ballot pack at the Electoral Office for Northern Ireland.

Replace a lost or damaged ballot paper

Your ballot paper needs to clearly display your details and voting choice. If it has been damaged, you’ll need to get another one.

You cannot vote at a polling station if you registered to vote by post but your ballot paper was then lost or damaged.

In England, Scotland and Wales

You can either:

  • ask your electoral registration office to post a replacement
  • collect a replacement from your electoral registration office up to 5pm on election day

In Northern Ireland

You cannot replace a lost ballot paper in Northern Ireland.

If your ballot paper has been damaged, you must return it with the declaration of identity form.

You can either:

  • ask the Electoral Office for Northern Ireland to post a replacement
  • collect a replacement from your Electoral Office for Northern Ireland by 5pm the day before election day
